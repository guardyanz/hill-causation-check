# Hill Causation Scorecard

**Claim:** Eating food Z → disease W

**Effect size:** One observational cohort study reports RR 1.3 (95% CI 1.05–1.6).

**Study design:** Single prospective cohort study; no RCT, no meta-analysis, no replication yet.

**Population and setting:** One adult population in a single country, followed after baseline exposure measurement.

**Source:** Single cohort study as reported in the headline (study citation to be added from the primary paper, not the news article). This example is illustrative: replace food Z and disease W with the real claim and the real study citation before using it as evidence.

## Scorecard

| # | Viewpoint | Verdict | Evidence (one line) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strength | Weak | RR 1.3 is a small effect that confounding or measurement error could plausibly explain. | Single cohort study, RR 1.3 (95% CI 1.05–1.6) |
| 2 | Consistency | Fail | No independent replication; a single study in one population. | No replication found at time of assessment |
| 3 | Specificity | Unknown | No evidence presented on whether Z is linked specifically to W. | Single cohort study |
| 4 | Temporality | Pass | Exposure was measured at baseline, before the outcome occurred. | Single cohort study design |
| 5 | Biological gradient (dose-response) | Unknown | The study reports one exposure level only; no dose-response gradient is shown. | Single cohort study |
| 6 | Plausibility | Weak | A mechanism is proposed but not demonstrated in humans. | Single cohort study discussion |
| 7 | Coherence | Unknown | No check against the known natural history of the disease is presented. | Single cohort study |
| 8 | Experiment | Unknown | No trial or cessation study; nobody has shown removing Z lowers risk. | No trial found |
| 9 | Analogy | Weak | A related food is loosely analogous, but no accepted causal analogue is cited. | Single cohort study discussion |

## Verdict

**Tier:** WEAK ASSOCIATION

**Pass count:** 1 / 9

**What would change this verdict:** Independent replication in a different population, a dose-response gradient across exposure levels, and ideally an RCT or a trial showing risk falls when Z is removed.

## Notes

- A single RR 1.3 from one observational study is the kind of result that routinely fails to replicate. Do not change behaviour on the basis of this alone.
- This scorecard judges strength of evidence for a general claim. It does not give personal medical advice. Medical decisions belong with a qualified clinician.
